Ball Battles

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 October 2026

This policy explains how information is handled when you play Ball Battles. It covers the standalone Android app and the browser version of the game. Their features differ, so the relevant sections below explain what applies to each version.

1. Who is responsible?

Ball Battles is maintained by ChickabeeJJ (the game developer). We do not operate an account database or gameplay server for the standalone Android app. For privacy questions, use the public Ball Battles GitHub Issues page.

Important: GitHub Issues are public. Do not post passwords, email addresses, account identifiers, precise location, or other sensitive personal information. You can open an issue asking how to make a privacy request without including the private details themselves. GitHub handles information posted to its services under its own Privacy Statement.

2. Standalone Android app

The current Google Play Android version runs without the CrazyGames SDK. It does not provide CrazyGames sign-in, CrazyGames advertising, or the game's online PvP/friend-room features.

The app saves gameplay information on your device using local app/web storage. This can include:

  • Game progress, coins, unlocked balls, mastery/progression, quest and reward progress, and battle statistics.
  • Your settings, selected game mode/map, team or ball selections, and other gameplay preferences.

This saved gameplay information is used to restore your progress and preferences. The Android game does not send these local save records to a server operated by the developer. Your device or operating-system provider may process technical information independently under its own terms.

Your saved game data stays on the device until the app's data/storage is cleared or the app is uninstalled. Removing the app may permanently remove local progress.

3. Browser version and CrazyGames

When the browser version is played in an environment that supports CrazyGames, it can load the CrazyGames SDK. CrazyGames may process technical/device and usage information and, depending on platform features and your account state, account information, gameplay events, saved progress, and advertising-related information.

The game uses available CrazyGames SDK features for gameplay lifecycle events, saving progress, checking the current player's username/profile where relevant, and requesting ads when the platform makes ads available. If you are signed in, game progress may be handled by CrazyGames' save service rather than only by your browser. CrazyGames controls its own processing and retention. Read the CrazyGames Privacy Policy for details.

These CrazyGames integrations do not run in the current standalone Android build.

4. Optional online multiplayer in the hosted version

In supported browser/CrazyGames environments, if you choose to use online PvP or a friend room, the game uses PeerJS-based networking to establish a connection with another player. Information needed for the match can include your displayed nickname (which may be your available CrazyGames username or the default name “Player”), selected ball/squad identifiers, map and match settings, match seed, aiming/gameplay updates, and results.

Network services involved in establishing and maintaining a connection may process connection metadata, such as IP address and technical connection information. The other player can see your displayed nickname and the match information needed to play. Do not use your real name or include private information in a player name. Online multiplayer is not available in the current standalone Android build.

PeerJS is a third-party networking component. We do not control its infrastructure or the data handling of internet/network providers. See PeerJS for information about the project.

5. Support requests and public content

If you create an issue on our GitHub Issues page, GitHub and the public may see the issue text, your GitHub username/profile information, and related technical metadata. Please keep the report general and do not submit private data. Issue content may remain publicly visible even after an issue is closed; GitHub controls its service and retention policies.

6. How information is used and shared

We use the information described above to run the game, save progress and settings, enable supported online matches, and respond to support requests. For the standalone Android app, gameplay saves remain on your device and are not uploaded to a developer-operated game server.

We do not sell your local Android gameplay save data. Where you use third-party services—such as Google Play, CrazyGames, PeerJS-related networking, or GitHub—those providers process information under their own terms and privacy notices. We cannot control their independent practices.

7. Security

For the standalone Android app, keeping the game save on your device avoids sending that save to a developer-operated server. For third-party services and networked browser play, information is handled by those services and transmitted over networks. No method of storing or transmitting information can be guaranteed completely secure, and we cannot guarantee the security of services operated by third parties.

8. Retention and deletion

  • Android saves: remain in local app/web storage until you clear app data/storage or uninstall the app.
  • Browser saves: may remain in browser storage until you clear site data; if the CrazyGames account/save service is active, CrazyGames' own retention and account controls apply.
  • Online match data: is exchanged to operate a live match; service providers may process connection metadata according to their own policies.
  • GitHub Issues: are public and subject to GitHub's content and retention controls. Closing an issue does not necessarily remove its public history.

9. Children and personal information

The standalone Android app does not ask players to create a developer account or enter personal contact information. Third-party platforms have their own minimum-age rules and privacy practices. Please do not include personal information in public issues, player names, or multiplayer messages.

10. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy if the game's features or data practices change. When we do, we will update the “Last updated” date on this page. Please review it periodically, especially after major game updates.

11. Privacy inquiries

For questions about this policy, use the Ball Battles GitHub Issues page. As noted above, issues are public, so please do not include sensitive personal information.
